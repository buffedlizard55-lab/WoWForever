Method · snapshot 2026-09-18

How this site verifies facts

The project brief was blunt: work line by line, verify against trusted sources, provide links for manual review, flag irregularities, and produce no hallucinations. This page is the method that implements it — and the log of everything we found that failed the test.

The line-by-line procedure

  1. Open the primary page. Prefer Blizzard; otherwise the outlet that conducted the interview; otherwise the guide site that did the datamining. Aggregators and “news” reposts are skipped.
  2. Quote or paraphrase exactly one statement at a time. Numbers, dates and proper nouns are copied character-for-character from the source, never from memory.
  3. Record it in the ledger (data/claims.js) with an ID, a category, a status, a snapshot date, and one or more source IDs.
  4. Separate fact from inference. If a sentence contains reasoning, it gets an ours marker and a visible explanation of the reasoning.
  5. Search for conflicts. A new claim is only accepted after checking whether it contradicts an existing one; conflicts are recorded as explicit rows with the resolution and the winner.
  6. Date-stamp and re-check. Every claim carries the date it was last verified. Beta claims are expected to churn; official launch facts are not.
  7. Leave the tombstone. Superseded numbers are kept in the ledger with a note, so reviewers can see what changed and when.

The procedure is enforced by machine, not by intention. Four offline tools run on every push and pull request (.github/workflows/verify.yml) and each one refuses a specific failure:

  • tools/check-ledger.mjs — a claim with no source, a source that does not exist, a duplicate id or a broken category fails the build; --stale 30 lists claims older than 30 days as a warning.
  • tools/check-citations.mjs — any external link that is not a registered source, any internal link or #anchor that does not resolve, any claim id printed in prose that is missing from the ledger, any unbalanced tag, a canonical link that does not name the page it sits on, or a sitemap.xml that disagrees with the page list fails the build.
  • tools/check-market-log.mjs — a market row that breaks the CSV schema, lacks an observer, or carries a date before 4 November 2026 fails the build: there is no auction house to observe yet.
  • tools/check-quotes.mjs — every quotation of three or more words must sit within 1400 characters of a source link or a claim id. A quotation that looks sourced but has no source next to it fails the build.

What the tools cannot do: they check that a quotation is anchored, never that its words match the live page. That is a human check, and it is the one that caught I-11, I-12 and I-13 in this session. Treating a green build as proof of accuracy would be exactly the kind of overclaiming this page exists to prevent.

What we refuse to publish

  • A class tier list or spec ranking for level 60. The data does not exist yet.
  • A BiS list with item names. Item stats are hidden until they drop, and all dungeon drops were re-itemised.
  • Gold-per-hour figures, price projections, or “buy this now” calls before a live auction house exists.
  • Routes, bots, exploits, gold buying, account sales, or GDKP organisation — the last is explicitly banned in Forever.
  • Rehosted content from Wowhead, Icy Veins or any other site. We summarise, link and credit.
  • Screenshots or quotes presented as Blizzard statements when they come from a press interview; the distinction is always shown.

Irregularity log

Findings that a reader should know about. Each entry states what was observed, why it is a problem, and what we do about it. This log is a first-class deliverable of the project.

I-1 · Fabricated ranking content is already being published Flagged

Multiple gold-selling and boosting sites publish complete PvP tier lists, spec rankings and Best-in-Slot lists for Forever, including level-60 rankings, while the beta is capped at level 20 and no level-60 item data exists. Several combine genuinely accurate facts (quoted from Blizzard and Wowhead) with invented rankings, which makes the invention harder to spot. Some additionally assert an “arena strategy” for a game that has never announced arenas.

Our handling: these domains are registered in the source list with tier community, excluded from every claim about the game, and never linked. The only claims that reference them (C100, C101) use them as evidence that the irregularity exists — never as evidence about Forever.

I-2 · Unpublished PvP numbers are being monetised Flagged

At least one RMT-adjacent site advertises a multi-thousand-dollar “Rank 14 pre-order”, payable now and conditional on Blizzard shipping a Classic honor ladder. Honor rates, rank thresholds, titles and vendor prices are all unpublished.

Our handling: recorded in the ledger as C101 and treated as evidence of the risk, not as evidence about the game.

I-3 · Conflicting dungeon level ranges in circulation Resolved

Early coverage listed Krol'dok Stronghold as 40–55 (and in one table 35–40) and City of Dalaran as 20–35. Blizzard published a correction on 15 September 2026: Krol'dok is 40–45, and the official table settles Dalaran at 28–33.

Our handling: ledger row C102 keeps both the superseded and the corrected values, and the site quotes only the corrected bands. Note that Wowhead's own dungeon guide still shows the older numbers in its prose while its table is correct — a good example of why we cite the blue post.

I-4 · “No WoW Token” is a “no plans” statement, not a policy Press caveat

The no-Token, no-boost position comes from a BlizzCon interview (Clay Stone, with Nora Mills) rather than a published policy page, and the phrasing is explicitly “no plans”. One aggregator makes exactly this point.

Our handling: marked press everywhere, with the caveat printed next to the claim.

I-5 · Economy tooling does not yet support the game Verified status

TradeSkillMaster's current release supports Retail, MoP Classic, TBC Classic and Classic Era — there is no Forever build, and on vanilla-ruleset clients TSM's native price sources are limited, requiring an external price addon.

Our handling: stated prominently on the Gold page instead of pretending a TSM workflow exists. Re-checked at each update.

I-6 · Duplicated datamined achievement IDs Open question

Wowhead's datamine of Legacy point sources shows each Legacy achievement twice under two different IDs (for example 62014 and 63998 for the same Alchemy milestone). The cause is not documented.

Our handling: we cite the achievement names and effects, never the IDs, and keep this open until the duplication is explained.

I-7 · The new race is behind a paywall, which is easy to misstate Purchase gating

Forever is described — correctly — as included with a WoW subscription, and that has led secondary coverage to state flatly that “Forever is free”. The shop listing shows that the Skyborne race and the Zephras Isle starting experience require at least the Skyborne Heroic Pack ($29.99). Both statements are true at once, and the second one materially affects anyone planning to play Skyborne or to level with friends who want to.

Our handling: recorded as claim C002 and C109, stated on the Roadmap, Classes and Home pages, and labelled as a shop fact rather than a gameplay fact.

I-8 · Forever item pages can show drop data from other game versions Open question

Wowhead's Forever namespace is the only practical item database for this game, but the pages are aggregated: opening item 226404, Tarnished Undermine Real, shows “Added in patch 1.60.1” alongside a “Dropped by (133)” table whose filters include Season of Discovery difficulties (SoD Normal, Heat Level 1–3, Seasonal 40) and vanilla raid bosses such as Molten Core, Blackwing Lair, Zul'Gurub and Stratholme. Forever's own launch content list does not include those raids.

Our handling: a drop source is never quoted from a Forever item page alone. Every item entry on this site must be cross-checked against Forever-specific material (a Forever news post, a Forever guide, or in-game observation) before the acquisition line is published. Ledger claim C110.

I-9 · The mount price was reported two different ways Resolved by quoting both

Kotaku's BlizzCon report said riding training “costs more gold than before”. Two days later the Sportskeeda interview gave exact figures: “the training is the full first 100 or 1,000 gold depending on which level … and then you get your first mount for free”. Read carelessly, the two statements can produce three wrong claims — “mounts cost 1,000 gold”, “mounts are free”, “training is 100 gold”.

Our handling: the figure is always quoted with its tier (“100 or 1,000 gold depending on level”) and the 100 % / 1,000 g split is never presented as a single price; the earlier claim (C082) stays in the ledger with a cross-reference to C127 so the sequence is visible. We also do not publish a Classic-versus-Forever comparison of the total mount cost, because “more than before” was said about the training line item and no first-party Classic price page is registered here — an unsourced comparison would be a new invented number wearing a historical costume. Gold page, Catalysts table.

I-10 · Interview “what ifs” circulating as roadmap items Watch item

In the same interview a Blizzard designer said “I would love to have a Caverns of Time-themed battleground”, and others mentioned Gilneas and Uldum as places they would like to visit — each time with the explicit caveat that these are long-term wishes and “nothing to be promising”. Aggregation sites routinely drop the caveat.

Our handling: every one of those lines is recorded in the ledger with status Not announced (C140) and the caveat attached, and the PvP page states it next to the battleground section so a reader cannot mistake a wish for a plan. This is the same failure mode as I-1, caught one step earlier — before a false roadmap entry exists to be cited.

I-11 · A quotation on our own Gear page we could not find in its source Self-corrected

The Gear page attributed a sentence to Icy Veins' class guides — “WoW Forever has reworked itemization, so old Classic item lists cannot establish your best equipment” — as an italic quotation. Re-opening the Guides page in this session, that sentence was not in it, and no registered page carried it word for word. The meaning was supported by the guides' own scope statements, but a reader clicking through would not have found the quoted text, which is exactly the failure this project exists to prevent.

Our handling: the invented-looking quotation was deleted and replaced with three fragments that are on the page — “covers leveling and PvE play through level 20”, “starting recommendations based on the available WoW Forever trainer and talent information”, and “are early ratings based on the Level 20 toolkit and do not reflect end-game potential” — each read from the Arms Warrior guide on 18 September 2026. The Gear page states the correction inline so the change is visible rather than silent. Lesson applied: every future italic quotation must be copy-checked against the live page in the same pass that adds it; a paraphrase may be presented as a paraphrase, never as a quotation.

I-12 · Our own coverage audit was too pessimistic Self-corrected

This session's coverage claim (C139) recorded per-spec Forever guides as arriving “class by class”, with Warrior as the only verified set. That came from the Warrior hub page alone. Opening the Arms Warrior guide showed a class switcher covering Balance Druid, Beast Mastery Hunter, Frost Mage, Holy Paladin, Discipline Priest, Assassination Rogue, Elemental Shaman, Affliction Warlock and Arms Warrior, with Fury and Protection Warrior alongside — every class has at least one spec guide, and all of them are level-20/beta-scoped, with ratings the guide itself says “do not reflect end-game potential”.

Our handling: C139 was corrected in place with the correction recorded in its own note, the source entry now lists all eleven spec-guide URLs (with the honest note that only the Warrior overview and the Arms guide were read end to end), and the class reading list links each one with the level-20 caveat attached. The direction of this error matters: the first version understated what other sites publish. Understating is still wrong, and it is the kind of error a reader cannot detect — so it is logged here rather than quietly fixed.

I-13 · Secondary reporting was carrying first-party labels Self-corrected

The first provenance pass treated several secondary Q&A and news reports as Official because they were reporting Blizzard statements. That is too loose for an auditable site: a report can be accurate and still not be the primary source. The direct Blizzard Krol’dok clarification and Blizzard known-issues post are now registered, the affected rows and claims use Press where the evidence is secondary, and the source checker rejects an Official claim without a Blizzard first-party source.

Our handling: the secondary links remain visible for corroboration and manual review; they were not deleted. This preserves useful reporting without allowing its evidence class to imply first-party authority.

Corrections process

  1. Find the claim ID on the relevant page or in the ledger.
  2. Open an issue on the repository with: the claim ID, the corrected statement, and a link to a primary source.
  3. If the source is stronger than what we cited, the claim is updated and the previous version is retained in the ledger history.
  4. Every update bumps the snapshot date at the top of the page; nothing is edited silently.

Disagreements about our ours-marked analysis are welcome; those sections carry the reasoning so it can be argued with directly.
