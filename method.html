Method · snapshot 2026-09-19

How this site verifies facts

The project brief was blunt: work line by line, verify against trusted sources, provide links for manual review, flag irregularities, and produce no hallucinations. This page is the method that implements it — and the log of everything we found that failed the test.

The line-by-line procedure

  1. Open the primary page. Prefer Blizzard; otherwise the outlet that conducted the interview; otherwise the guide site that did the datamining. Aggregators and “news” reposts are skipped.
  2. Quote or paraphrase exactly one statement at a time. Numbers, dates and proper nouns are copied character-for-character from the source, never from memory.
  3. Record it in the ledger (data/claims.js) with an ID, a category, a status, a snapshot date, and one or more source IDs.
  4. Separate fact from inference. If a sentence contains reasoning, it gets an ours marker and a visible explanation of the reasoning.
  5. Search for conflicts. A new claim is only accepted after checking whether it contradicts an existing one; conflicts are recorded as explicit rows with the resolution and the winner.
  6. Date-stamp and re-check. Every claim carries the date it was last verified. Beta claims are expected to churn; official launch facts are not.
  7. Leave the tombstone. Superseded numbers are kept in the ledger with a note, so reviewers can see what changed and when.

The procedure is enforced by machine, not by intention. Five offline tools run on every push and pull request (.github/workflows/verify.yml), plus a sixth self-test, and each one refuses a specific failure:

  • tools/check-ledger.mjs — a claim with no source, a source that does not exist, a duplicate id or a broken category fails the build; --stale 30 lists claims older than 30 days as a warning.
  • tools/check-citations.mjs — any external link that is not a registered source, any internal link or #anchor that does not resolve, any claim id printed in prose that is missing from the ledger, any unbalanced tag, a canonical link that does not name the page it sits on, or a sitemap.xml that disagrees with the page list fails the build.
  • tools/check-market-log.mjs — a market row that breaks the CSV schema, lacks an observer, or carries a date before 4 November 2026 fails the build: there is no auction house to observe yet.
  • tools/check-quotes.mjs — every quotation of three or more words must sit within 1400 characters of a source link or a claim id. A quotation that looks sourced but has no source next to it fails the build.
  • tools/check-a11y.mjs — landmarks, one h1 with no skipped heading levels, labelled navigation that matches on every page, table captions and scoped headers, labelled controls, no duplicate ids, and the presence of focus styles, reduced-motion handling, print rules and sticky-header anchor offsets.
  • tools/test-source-watch.mjs — runs the source watcher against a fixture server to prove, offline, that a first run writes a baseline, an edited page is reported, a regression keeps its old fingerprint, and a runner that can reach nothing writes no baseline at all. It tests the checker, which is the part a green build would otherwise hide.

What the tools cannot do: they check that a quotation is anchored, never that its words match the live page, and they cannot read a source on your behalf. That is a human check, and it is the one that caught I-11, I-12 and I-13 in one session, I-14, I-15 and I-16 in the next, and I-17 and I-18 in the 19 September re-read of the main Blizzard articles — every one of them a fault on our own pages rather than in someone else's reporting. Treating a green build as proof of accuracy would be exactly the kind of overclaiming this page exists to prevent.

What we refuse to publish

  • A class tier list or spec ranking for level 60. The data does not exist yet.
  • A BiS list with item names. Item stats are hidden until they drop, and all dungeon drops were re-itemised.
  • Gold-per-hour figures, price projections, or “buy this now” calls before a live auction house exists.
  • Routes, bots, exploits, gold buying, account sales, or GDKP organisation — the last is explicitly banned in Forever.
  • Rehosted content from Wowhead, Icy Veins or any other site. We summarise, link and credit.
  • Screenshots or quotes presented as Blizzard statements when they come from a press interview; the distinction is always shown.
  • Anything behind a sign-up, a paywall or a key-gated API — including Blizzard's own developer APIs. Every source on this site is a public page any reader can open without registering; that is why there is no automated price or character feed.
  • A fact borrowed from a page we could not read. If a source blocks automated retrieval, the limitation goes on the source and into the irregularity log — it is never filled in from a summary without saying so.

Irregularity log

Findings that a reader should know about. Each entry states what was observed, why it is a problem, and what we do about it. This log is a first-class deliverable of the project.

I-1 · Fabricated ranking content is already being published Flagged

Multiple gold-selling and boosting sites publish complete PvP tier lists, spec rankings and Best-in-Slot lists for Forever, including level-60 rankings, while the beta is capped at level 20 and no level-60 item data exists. Several combine genuinely accurate facts (quoted from Blizzard and Wowhead) with invented rankings, which makes the invention harder to spot. Some additionally assert an “arena strategy” for a game that has never announced arenas.

Our handling: these domains are registered in the source list with tier community, excluded from every claim about the game, and never linked. The only claims that reference them (C100, C101) use them as evidence that the irregularity exists — never as evidence about Forever.

I-2 · Unpublished PvP numbers are being monetised Flagged

At least one RMT-adjacent site advertises a multi-thousand-dollar “Rank 14 pre-order”, payable now and conditional on Blizzard shipping a Classic honor ladder. Honor rates, rank thresholds, titles and vendor prices are all unpublished.

Our handling: recorded in the ledger as C101 and treated as evidence of the risk, not as evidence about the game.

I-3 · Conflicting dungeon level ranges in circulation Resolved

Early coverage listed Krol'dok Stronghold as 40–55 (and in one table 35–40) and City of Dalaran as 20–35. Blizzard published a correction on 15 September 2026: Krol'dok is 40–45, and the official table settles Dalaran at 28–33.

Our handling: ledger row C102 keeps both the superseded and the corrected values, and the site quotes only the corrected bands. Note that Wowhead's own dungeon guide still shows the older numbers in its prose while its table is correct — a good example of why we cite the blue post.

I-4 · “No WoW Token” is a “no plans” statement, not a policy Press caveat

The no-Token, no-boost position comes from a BlizzCon interview (Clay Stone, with Nora Mills) rather than a published policy page, and the phrasing is explicitly “no plans”. One aggregator makes exactly this point.

Our handling: marked press everywhere, with the caveat printed next to the claim.

I-5 · Economy tooling does not yet support the game Verified status

TradeSkillMaster's current release supports Retail, MoP Classic, TBC Classic and Classic Era — there is no Forever build, and on vanilla-ruleset clients TSM's native price sources are limited, requiring an external price addon.

Updated 19 September 2026: the blanket form of this entry was too broad. Auctionator release 337 (17 September 2026) declares “Supported Classic Forever patch1.60.1”, making it the first auction-house add-on this project has verified as Forever-capable; TradeSkillMaster, Questie and Details! still list no Forever build. The status is now tracked row by row, with dates, on Add-ons.

Our handling: the original wording is kept above rather than deleted, so the change is visible. TSM-specific statements on the Gold page stay as they are, because they are still true; general statements were narrowed to name which add-on they are about.

I-6 · Duplicated datamined achievement IDs Open question

Wowhead's datamine of Legacy point sources shows each Legacy achievement twice under two different IDs (for example 62014 and 63998 for the same Alchemy milestone). The cause is not documented.

Our handling: we cite the achievement names and effects, never the IDs, and keep this open until the duplication is explained.

I-7 · The new race is behind a paywall, which is easy to misstate Purchase gating

Forever is described — correctly — as included with a WoW subscription, and that has led secondary coverage to state flatly that “Forever is free”. The shop listing shows that the Skyborne race and the Zephras Isle starting experience require at least the Skyborne Heroic Pack ($29.99). Both statements are true at once, and the second one materially affects anyone planning to play Skyborne or to level with friends who want to.

Our handling: recorded as claim C002 and C109, stated on the Roadmap, Classes and Home pages, and labelled as a shop fact rather than a gameplay fact.

I-8 · Forever item pages can show drop data from other game versions Open question

Wowhead's Forever namespace is the only practical item database for this game, but the pages are aggregated: opening item 226404, Tarnished Undermine Real, shows “Added in patch 1.60.1” alongside a “Dropped by (133)” table whose filters include Season of Discovery difficulties (SoD Normal, Heat Level 1–3, Seasonal 40) and vanilla raid bosses such as Molten Core, Blackwing Lair, Zul'Gurub and Stratholme. Forever's own launch content list does not include those raids.

Our handling: a drop source is never quoted from a Forever item page alone. Every item entry on this site must be cross-checked against Forever-specific material (a Forever news post, a Forever guide, or in-game observation) before the acquisition line is published. Ledger claim C110.

I-9 · The mount price was reported two different ways Resolved by quoting both

Kotaku's BlizzCon report said riding training “costs more gold than before”. Two days later the Sportskeeda interview gave exact figures: “the training is the full first 100 or 1,000 gold depending on which level … and then you get your first mount for free”. Read carelessly, the two statements can produce three wrong claims — “mounts cost 1,000 gold”, “mounts are free”, “training is 100 gold”.

Our handling: the figure is always quoted with its tier (“100 or 1,000 gold depending on level”) and the 100 % / 1,000 g split is never presented as a single price; the earlier claim (C082) stays in the ledger with a cross-reference to C127 so the sequence is visible. We also do not publish a Classic-versus-Forever comparison of the total mount cost, because “more than before” was said about the training line item and no first-party Classic price page is registered here — an unsourced comparison would be a new invented number wearing a historical costume. Gold page, Catalysts table.

I-10 · Interview “what ifs” circulating as roadmap items Watch item

In the same interview a Blizzard designer said “I would love to have a Caverns of Time-themed battleground”, and others mentioned Gilneas and Uldum as places they would like to visit — each time with the explicit caveat that these are long-term wishes and “nothing to be promising”. Aggregation sites routinely drop the caveat.

Our handling: every one of those lines is recorded in the ledger with status Not announced (C140) and the caveat attached, and the PvP page states it next to the battleground section so a reader cannot mistake a wish for a plan. This is the same failure mode as I-1, caught one step earlier — before a false roadmap entry exists to be cited.

I-11 · A quotation on our own Gear page we could not find in its source Self-corrected

The Gear page attributed a sentence to Icy Veins' class guides — “WoW Forever has reworked itemization, so old Classic item lists cannot establish your best equipment” — as an italic quotation. Re-opening the Guides page in this session, that sentence was not in it, and no registered page carried it word for word. The meaning was supported by the guides' own scope statements, but a reader clicking through would not have found the quoted text, which is exactly the failure this project exists to prevent.

Our handling: the invented-looking quotation was deleted and replaced with three fragments that are on the page — “covers leveling and PvE play through level 20”, “starting recommendations based on the available WoW Forever trainer and talent information”, and “are early ratings based on the Level 20 toolkit and do not reflect end-game potential” — each read from the Arms Warrior guide on 18 September 2026. The Gear page states the correction inline so the change is visible rather than silent. Lesson applied: every future italic quotation must be copy-checked against the live page in the same pass that adds it; a paraphrase may be presented as a paraphrase, never as a quotation.

I-12 · Our own coverage audit was too pessimistic Self-corrected

This session's coverage claim (C139) recorded per-spec Forever guides as arriving “class by class”, with Warrior as the only verified set. That came from the Warrior hub page alone. Opening the Arms Warrior guide showed a class switcher covering Balance Druid, Beast Mastery Hunter, Frost Mage, Holy Paladin, Discipline Priest, Assassination Rogue, Elemental Shaman, Affliction Warlock and Arms Warrior, with Fury and Protection Warrior alongside — every class has at least one spec guide, and all of them are level-20/beta-scoped, with ratings the guide itself says “do not reflect end-game potential”.

Our handling: C139 was corrected in place with the correction recorded in its own note, the source entry now lists all eleven spec-guide URLs (with the honest note that only the Warrior overview and the Arms guide were read end to end), and the class reading list links each one with the level-20 caveat attached. The direction of this error matters: the first version understated what other sites publish. Understating is still wrong, and it is the kind of error a reader cannot detect — so it is logged here rather than quietly fixed.

I-13 · Secondary reporting was carrying first-party labels Self-corrected

The first provenance pass treated several secondary Q&A and news reports as Official because they were reporting Blizzard statements. That is too loose for an auditable site: a report can be accurate and still not be the primary source. The direct Blizzard Krol’dok clarification and Blizzard known-issues post are now registered, the affected rows and claims use Press where the evidence is secondary, and the source checker rejects an Official claim without a Blizzard first-party source.

Our handling: the secondary links remain visible for corroboration and manual review; they were not deleted. This preserves useful reporting without allowing its evidence class to imply first-party authority.

I-14 · Our own page claimed a source we had not registered Self-corrected

The Work plan page stated that the IGN Nordic interview summary was “registered” during the previous session. The source registry contained no IGN Nordic entry at all: the interview was being quoted through a Wowhead summary that was itself unregistered, and the sentence describing our own registry was simply wrong. This is the failure mode the project is built to catch, committed on our own pages rather than in someone else's reporting.

Two checks now make this class of error visible instead of silent: the citation tool verifies every number a page prints about this project against the data files, and it fails the build if method.html does not document every evidence class that the registry or ledger actually uses — which is exactly how the missing tooling class was caught.

Our handling: two sources were registered — ign-nordic (the interview itself, whose direct retrieval returned HTTP 403, a limitation recorded on the source and repeated whenever it is cited) and wh-ign-nordic (Wowhead's summary, read end to end). The wrong sentence was replaced and the correction recorded as claim C158. Every sentence quoted on this site from that interview was cross-checked against the Dutch edition of the same interview, and no sentence is quoted that does not appear in both.

Status, 19 September 2026 (third session): a fifth direct fetch of the English page returned HTTP 403. The same day, the project's own weekly watcher — running on a GitHub Actions runner at 00:20 UTC — fetched the page without error and recorded a 7,450-character text fingerprint (tools/source-state.json, entry ign-nordic, zero failures). So the page is live and is served to a plain client; the refusal is specific to the reading path used here. That narrows the limitation without closing it: nobody on this project has yet read the live page, so the indexed-text caveat stays on every citation (C218). The Sportskeeda interview is the mirror case — readable here, refused to the runner — which is why a 403 from one client is never treated as evidence about a page.

I-15 · A guide's expectation was stated with first-party confidence Self-corrected

The home page described the level cap as “level 20 (level 30 after two weeks)”, and the Classes page said the cap “rises to level 30 after two weeks”. Blizzard has confirmed the cap starts at 20 and rises to 30 during the test, but the two-week timing is Wowhead's expectation, published in a guide that says the content schedule “has not yet been confirmed”. Mixing the two made our pages sound more certain than the evidence behind them.

Our handling: both pages now state the official fact (“level 20, then 30 later in the beta”) and cite Blizzard's beta article. The timing moved to Beta log, attributed to Wowhead and labelled an expectation (claim C154). This is the same discipline applied to interview “what ifs” in I-10, one step earlier.

I-16 · Add-on release lists were labelled as datamined client data Self-corrected

The two TradeSkillMaster distribution listings were registered with the datamine evidence class, which this site defines as data extracted from the beta client. They are nothing of the kind: they are a project's own release metadata. The label overstated the evidence and blurred two very different things — what a game build contains, and what a piece of software says about itself.

Our handling: a tooling class now exists, the two TSM listings and all four new add-on listings carry it, claim C093 was reclassified, and the ledger checker refuses a tooling claim that cites no tooling source. The add-on tracker uses the class for exactly one purpose: statements about software versions, never statements about the game.

I-17 · “Indefinitely” was attributed to a Blizzard page that does not contain the word Self-corrected

Claim C004 and the home page's level-cap card said the cap stays at 60 “indefinitely” and cited only Blizzard's written What's Next recap. Re-reading that recap line by line on 19 September found no level-cap figure and no “indefinitely”: it frames content inside “the level 1–60 journey” and calls Barrow Deeps a “maximum-level” raid. The word comes from Executive Producer Holly Longdale's opening-ceremony remarks, as reported by PCGamesN (“Longdale says the level cap will stay at 60 ‘indefinitely’”); Kotaku separately quotes Tim Jones: “we have a level 60 cap. We are going to continue to build content horizontally.” The fact is well attested; the attribution was wrong, and it dressed a reported quotation as recap text.

Our handling: C004 now carries only what the recap says (official), the quotation lives in a new press row C160 with the reporter's link, PCGamesN is registered as a source, and the home-page card says which is which. The previous wording is preserved in C004's note and in C166.

I-18 · “No character transfer between rulesets” overstated a spoken answer Self-corrected

Claim C024 and two sentences on the PvP page said there is “no character transfer between rulesets”. The Q&A liveblog's sentence is “Without a character transfer, you can't swap between rulesets” — a swap needs a transfer, which does not say transfers do not exist. Blizzard's own Deep Dive recap names one such path in writing: a Hardcore character that dies “can transfer to another ruleset, including PvP”. Our paraphrase turned a conditional into a prohibition.

Our handling: C024 restated with the liveblog's exact sentence and the Hardcore transfer path; the PvP page's ruleset table note and preparation checklist reworded; whether transfers between the non-Hardcore rulesets will be offered is recorded as not announced. Found only by re-reading the source next to the claim — the same procedure that found I-17, applied during the 19 September pass to the five Blizzard pages the ledger leans on most.

I-19 · Unofficial “beta tracker” sites publish specific numbers no established outlet corroborates Flagged

Once the beta client became dataminable, a family of unofficial sites began publishing precise reads of it — a Darkspear Islands level 30–60 range joining the random battleground pool with Warsong Gulch and Arathi Basin, “Hyjal Crater” and “Mak'gora Arena” queues, a 15,000 Honor Points cap, PvP set piece counts. The sites are careful to label their data “datamined, may change”, which makes them look exactly like Wowhead's datamine coverage. The difference is provenance: no established outlet had published the same reads when this was checked on 19 September, and one tracker's “Battle for Gilneas (rated)” queue string directly contradicts the developer statement that there will not be rated PvP (C021) — the signature of legacy client data being read as new content, the same trap as I-8.

Our handling: the flag-unofficial-wikis entry was extended to name the pattern and the new domains, and claim C184 records it as community-tier with the promotion rule: any of these numbers may be adopted only once a reputable datamine or Blizzard publishes it. Recorded during the 19 September live-web pass.

I-20 · Set bonuses were datamined one day into a beta whose item data Blizzard said would stay hidden Datamine

Blizzard told MMORPG (18 September) that item data is obfuscated so “you will not see item stats until the item drops for you or on your server”, with no Dungeon Journal and a “Wild West” first raid week before BiS lists can exist. On 16–17 September — before the beta servers even opened — Wowhead published hundreds of set bonuses extracted from the beta client, including the new raid tier sets and the classic PvP rank sets. The two statements do not actually collide as written: the MMORPG statement is about item stats staying hidden until a drop, while what was datamined is set bonuses — a different kind of record. Blizzard has not explained publicly how the obfuscation separates the two, so this site asserts no mechanism; what matters is that no item stat, drop source or drop rate is in the set-bonus data. The juxtaposition still invites two wrong readings — that Blizzard's hidden-items promise has failed, or that a complete gear picture now exists. Neither is true. Wowhead's article carries a second warning of its own: “some effects from Season of Discovery have also appeared in our datamining, though we have taken care to filter them out of this article to the best of our ability” — the same shared-client-lineage contamination as I-8, this time acknowledged by the outlet itself.

Our handling: the datamine is registered (wh-setbonuses) and summarised at datamine tier with its snapshot date (C188, C189); no set bonus is treated as final, nothing from it feeds a BiS or gearing conclusion while item stats remain hidden, and the SoD-contamination caveat is recorded on the Gear page and as a false positive in the Beta log. Any third-party “complete BiS list” built from set bonuses alone is missing exactly the item-level data that is still hidden (see I-1). Recorded during the 19 September queue-closing pass.

I-21 · Blizzard's own pages label the same beta window with two different timezones Official

The pre-purchase article says the beta “begins September 17, 2026, PST, and is available through October 21, 2026, PST”. The Collector's Edition article, published the same week, prints the same window as the “access period of September 17 through October 21 PDT”. Both sentences are Blizzard's, about the same window, one day apart. The date is not in dispute and this site does not rewrite it; what differs is the zone label. Our reading — and it is our reading, not a Blizzard statement — is that the Collector's Edition label is right for a window that sits entirely inside US daylight time, that the pre-purchase article's PST is loose, and that PST is correct for the 4 November launch time because US daylight time ends on 1 November 2026. Recorded because a reader comparing the two Blizzard pages will see the difference, and because pretending it is not there is exactly the kind of tidying this project exists to avoid.

Our handling: both pages are registered (bnet-prepurchase, bnet-collectors-edition) and the discrepancy is a claim of its own (C204) rather than a silent correction in either direction. The site's own rule follows from it: a date is printed with the zone label its source printed, and never with a label this project added to make two pages agree — the same discipline as I-17, applied to a discrepancy between two first-party pages instead of between a page and a summary. Found during the 19 September endpoint-and-pattern pass.

I-22 · For months this site said no credential-free source could exist, without ever testing it Self-corrected

The limitations list carried a row headed “No credentials by design”, which explained that because the brief rules out API keys that need a sign-up, “there is no automated price or character data feed”. The first half of that is correct, and its citation is now stronger than it was: Blizzard's own Getting Started page documents the account, the mandatory authenticator and the OAuth client a caller must have before the first request is served (C194). The second half was an assumption wearing the clothes of a finding. It implied that nothing machine-readable is available at all, when two Blizzard-hosted services answer a plain request with no credential whatsoever — the forum's topic feed and the build manifest (C190–C192). Nobody had checked, so the row was true by default rather than by test, which is the same failure mode as I-17 and I-18 one level up: a statement about our own coverage that no one had verified.

Our handling: the endpoint register (data/apis.js, published on Public endpoints with its own checker) now records each candidate, the date it was fetched, what came back, what it may be used for and what it must never be used for — including the two rejected candidates and the one left deliberately unknown because its documentation could not be read. Three candidates failed this pass (Raider.IO on its terms and its scope, Blizzard's developer APIs on Blizzard's own requirement, Warcraft Logs on unreadability) and are published with the evidence for the refusal. The Work plan limitation row has been rewritten to name what is now watchable and what remains impossible. Recorded during the 19 September pass.

I-23 · Blizzard's umbrella page prints the launch time as PDT in one paragraph and PST in another Official

The Carve a New Path page opens with “launches November 4 at 3:00 p.m. PDT globally” and, a few paragraphs later, says the game “launches globally on November 4, 2026, at 3:00 p.m. PST”. The What's Next recap prints PST. Unlike I-21, where the window sat inside daylight time and the labels differed by name only, here the labels differ by an hour on the day in question: US daylight time ends on 1 November 2026, so on 4 November the Pacific zone is on standard time — 3:00 p.m. PST is 23:00 UTC, and 3:00 p.m. PDT would be 22:00 UTC. Two of three printings say PST and PST is the zone actually in force, so this site prints “3:00 p.m. PST (23:00 UTC)” — and says so, rather than quietly choosing.

Our handling: recorded as claim C214 with both Blizzard pages registered (bnet-carve-path, bnet-whats-next); the home page's launch card carries the UTC time and a link here. The exact minute is Blizzard's to confirm in a launch-day article; the weekly watcher fingerprints both pages, so an edit that removes the discrepancy will be noticed. Found during the 19 September third-session re-read of the blue-tracker items.

I-24 · Two Blizzard recaps describe the same transmog system as “opt-in” and as something you “opt out” of Official

The What's Next recap lists “opt-in Transmog” among its player-choice options. The Deep Dive recap, published a day later, describes visiting a transmogrifier to “configure transmogrified outfits or disable the system entirely”, says that “opting out means players will only see others around them as they are”, and that choosing Classic Mode at first launch disables it automatically. The two pages agree on every mechanic — the system can be on or off per player, Classic Mode turns it off, and appearance collection continues either way — and disagree on the one word that says which state a player on the modern preset starts in. Recorded because this site's Gear page says “opt-out”, following the Deep Dive's more detailed description, and a reader who opens the What's Next recap will find the other word.

Our handling: the discrepancy is noted on claim C210 and on the What's Next source entry rather than resolved by us; the Gear page keeps the Deep Dive's description because it is the page that actually describes the mechanism. If Blizzard's launch documentation states the default plainly, the claim is superseded with a new date. Found during the same 19 September re-read.

Corrections process

  1. Find the claim ID on the relevant page or in the ledger.
  2. Open an issue on the repository with: the claim ID, the corrected statement, and a link to a primary source.
  3. If the source is stronger than what we cited, the claim is updated and the previous version is retained in the ledger history.
  4. Every update bumps the snapshot date at the top of the page; nothing is edited silently.
