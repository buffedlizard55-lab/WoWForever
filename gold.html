Gold & markets · snapshot 2026-09-19

Gold-making in WoW Forever: the verified market map

A fresh Classic economy with no WoW Token, no GDKP, no paid boosts, an aggressive anti-gold-buyer policy, a rebuilt profession system, newly published mount-training prices (100 g, then 1,000 g) and a raid tier landing on 9 December. This page lists the verified mechanics that will move prices, dates them, and states honestly where tooling does not yet exist.

Tooling reality check before anything else.

TradeSkillMaster does not yet ship a Forever build. The current release (v4.14.77, 13 Sep 2026) declares support for Retail 12.1.0, MoP Classic, Burning Crusade Classic and Classic Era 1.15.9 — there is no 1.60.x / Forever entry. Wago addon listing On vanilla-ruleset clients, TSM's native price sources are also limited; market values require an external price addon such as Auctionator, Auctioneer or AHDB. Price Answer docs

Auctionator is the exception, and it arrived on 17 September 2026: release 337 declares “Supported Classic Forever patch1.60.1”, so an auction-house tool now exists for the beta client even though the market operations suite does not. Economy rules that define the market

These are the structural facts a gold-maker has to model first. They change what strategies are legal, what competition exists and how much gold enters the economy.

Economy rules that define the market. Columns: Rule, Verified detail, Market consequence, Evidence.
RuleVerified detailMarket consequenceEvidence
No GDKP GDKP is not allowed in Forever. The Q&A report attributes the reasons to Blizzard: it overtakes guild structures, prices players out of content, and launders gold; Blizzard also says it has been too lenient on gold buyers and will crack down aggressively. Removes a major gold-concentration channel and a whole class of “carry for gold” business. Demand shifts toward guild DKP/loot systems and open-market goods. Press Wowhead / Q&A report
No WoW Token, no boosts (planned) Clay Stone (Destin interview): no plans for a WoW Token or character boosts; “the journey is the game”; everyone starts at level 1. Gold cannot be bought officially, so gold velocity is driven by farming and trade alone. Also caps “pay to skip” demand that would otherwise monetise leveling. Press Warcraft TavernMassivelyOP
One US-wide auction house; Oceanic shares it Oceanic players are hosted in Oceania but “share the same US-wide auction house”. Names are region-wide; realms are replaced by rulesets. A much larger, deeper market than a single Classic realm: better liquidity, faster price discovery, and arbitrage becomes a matter of travel time rather than of which realm you picked. Press Q&A liveblog
Mount cost sits in the training The gold cost is the training, quoted by the developers as “100 or 1,000 gold depending on which level”, and the first mount is then free. Cosmetic bundle mounts give no advantage because the training still has to be bought. The structure is first-party: Blizzard's Deep Dive recap describes “adopting the Burning Crusade-style mount-cost approach, shifting the up-front mount cost into training and granting a mount when players learn the riding skill”; the figures are interview statements only. A major planned per-character gold sink, hitting every character twice — once near the level-40 mount tier and again at the level-60 tier. Early gold may retain value because the first wave arrives well after launch. Official structure · Press figures Blizzard Deep DiveSportskeeda interviewWowhead summaryKotaku
No summoning stones Meeting Stones will not summon players; a new LFG feature is intended for listing and discovering groups, and groups “make your way across to each other if you don't have a Warlock”. Travel and summons keep value. Warlocks gain a standing social/economic role; time-per-run rises, which slows farm throughput for everyone — and every gathering profession stays in contact with the world for longer per session. Press KotakuWowhead summary
No permanent retirement of content The interview reports that Blizzard does not plan content that becomes permanently unavailable, and that world events will still run; every raid is meant to keep a reason to return, including rare chase items on every boss. The itemisation interview adds the item-level half of the same idea: older-raid pieces stay worth keeping (the Onslaught Girdle) and biome/creature-type bonuses are meant to bring players “back to older content”. Legacy materials stay sellable for longer: the content that consumes them does not get switched off at the end of a phase. Slower decay than a Classic realm, where the previous tier is abandoned. Press Sportskeeda interviewIGN Nordic
Bigger banks, same bags Default bank space is two to four times larger; bags start at Classic size. Holding inventory is cheaper than in Classic, so “buy and hold” strategies cost less — but bag space during farming remains a real constraint. Press Wowhead / MMORPG
Addons: modern WoW's rules Addons are supported, and Forever “shares Mainline WoW's UI architecture, including the vast majority of APIs available in 12.1.5”, with the Midnight addon disarmament changes “including secrets” active as well. Addons that computationally automate marking or communicating are out, and there is no one-button rotation. A built-in damage meter and cooldown manager ship with the game, and every add-on has to be rebuilt against the new API. Automated auction-house behaviour is constrained; manual efficiency and knowledge keep more of an edge than macro automation. The API parity also makes a modern addon build the closest technical starting point — the addon still has to declare support for the game version. Press Icy VeinsKotakuWowhead / WoW UI Discord

4 Nov 2026 — launch A completely fresh economy: everyone starts at level 1 with no carried gold, no carried materials, and no WoW Token to inject supply. Realms are rulesets; the AH is US-wide. Extreme scarcity pricing for anything that accelerates leveling, plus a much deeper bid side than a single Classic realm. Early gathering is king.

A dated window to watch, not a quantity (C164).
Launch → level 40+ Riding training is the big gold sink: 100 gold at the first mount tier and 1,000 gold at the higher tier, with the first mount itself free once training is paid. The training-not-mount structure is confirmed in Blizzard's own recap; the figures are from the interview. Furthermore, class mounts (Forsaken Paladin Summon Warhorse/Charger) and edition/racial mounts (Cerulean Prideclaw, Galestriders) still require in-game riding training purchases (C162, C176). Blizzard Deep DivePre-PurchaseWowhead Mounts GuideSportskeeda interview Gold demand spikes as the population crosses into each mount tier; consumable and material prices compete against training savings for the same wallet. Two predictable demand collisions instead of one, affecting every player regardless of edition or class.
Launch → all levels Healing potions are crafted by First Aid, not Alchemy, and the recipes consume herbs, vials and cooking spices. The reported ladder is Minor 55, Lesser 85, Healing 135, Greater 155, Superior 215 and Major 275 First Aid skill (the last three from manuals), and Icy Veins expects “most, if not all” classes to train First Aid now that it makes emergency healing. Icy Veins report Spice and vial demand rises; Alchemy's monopoly on emergency healing ends. Expect a different price curve for pots than Classic players remember.
Launch → all levels Campsite objects grant buffs equivalent to class buffs and do not stack with them; every profession has three objects learned at different skill levels, with the first at skill 20 and advanced ones from Blueprint recipes that drop from dungeon bosses. Wowhead's Camping guide details the perk table: Blacksmithing (Sharpening Wheel: +Strength), Enchanting (Enchanted Lute: +Armor, All Stats, Resistances), Skinning (Camp Chair: +2% Crit), Fishing (Camp Bowl: +8% stats), Herbalism (Incense Candle: +Intellect), Mining (Lodestone: +melee AP), Tailoring (Faction Banner: +Spirit), and Leatherworking (Camp Tent: Rested XP) (C168). BlizzardWowhead Camping Guide Buffs become a crafted good sold through camps. Materials for the popular camp objects become a recurring consumable demand across all professions, and the class-buff overlap caps how much players will pay for equivalent effects.
Launch → all levels Crafted food grants a small XP bonus plus stats. Blizzard Leveling food becomes a mass-market consumable rather than a niche buff, with the caveat that Blizzard only says “small” bonus.
Launch → level 60 Dungeon mob XP was tuned down and dungeon quest XP up, with the overall curve unchanged. Wowhead Fewer repeat clears per level means fewer raw materials entering the market from dungeon grinding, and more value in questing zones. Boost-style dungeon farming is discouraged by design.
9 Dec 2026 — raids unlock Barrow Deeps (10p) and Hyjal Summit (20p) open; tier sets appear; a legendary reward is teased; no attunements gate entry. Blizzard The first genuine consumable spike: no attunement gate means raiders can funnel straight into the raid, so demand hits immediately rather than trickling in over weeks.
9 Dec onward — raid phase Crafted tier sets require Malleable Essence of Nature, obtainable only by disenchanting items from Hyjal Summit or Barrow Deeps; crafted pieces are Bind on Equip; one equip slot initially, two with Guardians of Hyjal reputation. Icy Veins datamine A two-sided market: raiders sell disenchant output, crafters buy it. Because crafted pieces are BoE, the finished item is tradeable — the clearest new high-value crafting market in the game.
Ongoing Legacy perks include increased Scarce materials from Mining/Herbalism/Skinning, and a perk that reduces or removes reagent costs; gathering-profession points come from reaching 150/225/300. BlizzardWowhead Late-cycle material supply increases as players invest in Legacy perks, which historically compresses raw-material prices over time.
Raid phase onward — chase items Every raid boss carries a few much lower drop-chance items that beat its normal drops, and items from earlier tiers are intended to stay useful — including through environment (forest/mountain/underground) and creature-type (Worgen/Dwarves/Undead/Dragons) effects. Sportskeeda interview Raid demand does not end when a group has its tier set: the same bosses keep being farmed, so consumable, food, enchant and disenchant-material demand stays higher for longer than a one-shot tier would produce.
Ongoing — nothing is retired The interview reports Blizzard saying it does not plan content that becomes permanently unavailable: “we never plan to do something like what was in Naxxramas, where you can't do it anymore”. Sportskeeda interview Old-tier materials and items are less likely to be dumped at the end of a phase, because the content that consumes them keeps running. Slower price decay on legacy consumables than on a Classic server.
Each PvP cycle — rank 5 Wowhead's datamine of the 14-rank Honor track lists “Access to Combat Potions Unlocked” at rank 5, with elite armour upgrades from rank 8 and the weapon arsenal at rank 14. Wowhead warns the ranks and rewards are subject to change. Wowhead datamine Combat potions become a PvP-progression-gated item, so consumable demand gains a second, seasonal driver on top of raiding. Every new PvP cycle re-opens that demand.
Ongoing / endgame Tarnished Undermine Reals return as a dungeon currency; in SoD they bought gear, toys, patterns and reagents; acquisition in Forever is not yet known. Wowhead A vendor gear path competes directly with auction-house prices for those slots. Its effect depends entirely on the (unpublished) vendor list.

Where the opportunity sits, phase by phase Our reading

Each card names the verified mechanic it rests on. The interpretation — what it means for a seller — is ours and is labelled as such; the mechanic is sourced in the catalyst table above.

Launch week (4 Nov)

A brand-new economy with no Token and no carried-over stock: everyone starts at level 1 with empty bags. Verified basis: no Token/boosts planned, everyone starts fresh, one US-wide auction house. Read: raw materials and leveling consumables are the first thing any market can price, and gathering professions ramp earlier than in Classic because starter manuals come from a level 3–4 quest.

Leveling window (Nov, all levels)

Healing potions move from Alchemy to First Aid and consume herbs, vials and Mild/Hot Spices; crafted food carries a small XP bonus; campsite objects are crafted and sold as buffs that do not stack with class buffs. Read: the consumable basket is different from Classic's, so Classic-era price intuition is a liability.

Mount window (level 40+ and 60)

Training costs 100 gold at the first tier and 1,000 gold at the higher tier, and the mount is free once training is paid. Read: a predictable, population-wide gold sink competes with consumable spending at two moments, and a free epic mount also exists on the Legacy track at 55 points — worth knowing before you commit 1,000 gold to training.

Pre-raid (Nov 4 – Dec 9)

Nine dungeons, re-itemised loot, hundreds of new rare drops, Blueprint recipes from dungeon bosses, quest rewards widened for casters and healers, dungeon mob XP tuned down with quest XP up. Read: dungeon farming is less efficient for XP, so fewer repeat clears feed the market — and Blueprint recipes gate the camp-object market behind dungeon runs.

Raid week (9 Dec)

Barrow Deeps and Hyjal Summit open with no attunements; crafted tier sets need Malleable Essence of Nature from disenchanting those raids' items; crafted pieces are Bind on Equip. Read: the sharpest demand spike in the first phase, and the first genuinely new crafting market in the game.

Every PvP cycle

A new 14-rank Honor track each cycle; combat potions are unlocked at rank 5 in the datamine; armour upgrades arrive from rank 8 and weapons at rank 14. Read: PvP adds a recurring, seasonal consumable demand and a repeating gear grind for the same slots the auction house serves.

Professions and supply-side mechanics

What changed

  • System stays Classic: fixed material requirements, fixed stats on crafted items — the Dragonflight crafting overhaul is not used. Wowhead
  • 600+ new recipes across professions and level ranges. Blizzard
  • Crafted tier sets feed raid-tier gearing through raiding materials (see catalyst table).
  • Blueprint recipes come from dungeon bosses, not trainers. Blizzard
  • Starter gathering manuals are handed out by a level 3–4 quest, so gathering supply ramps earlier than in Classic. Wowhead
  • Supply-crate turn-ins exist again via the Azeroth Commerce Authority / Durotar Supply and Logistics. Wowhead

What is not published

  • Whether there are new professions or a changed skill cap. Nothing announced; assume Classic's 300 cap until told otherwise.
  • The recipe list, or how the 600+ recipes split across professions.
  • Which boss drops which Blueprint.
  • The exact size of the crafted-food XP bonus in Blizzard's own words. Two guide sites now print +5% (Icy Veins, and Wowhead's open-world article describing a “Well Fed” +5% Experience buff), so the number is corroborated analysis — but it is still not first-party, and it is labelled that way everywhere it appears (C091, C180).
  • Whether the profession slot limit remains two primary / three secondary.

Tooling: TSM status and how to prepare

Status of the standard tools Verified listing

  • TradeSkillMaster (TSM): latest build v4.14.77 (13 Sep 2026) lists Retail, MoP Classic, TBC Classic and Classic Era support. No Forever build. Wago listing · CurseForge page
  • Auctionator: release 337 (17 Sep 2026) declares “Supported Classic Forever patch1.60.1” — the first auction-house add-on verified here as Forever-capable. Wago listing
  • Questie and Details!: no Forever build listed on 19 Sep 2026, so quest routing and combat-log parsing are uncovered. Full tracker with dates →
  • TSM price data on vanilla-ruleset clients: native sources are limited to crafting and destroy value; market values require an external price addon (Auctionator, Auctioneer or AHDB). Price Answer documentation
  • Implication: plan on manual auction scanning with Auctionator for the first weeks of Forever — verify on the live client, because a version list declares support rather than proving it — and re-check TSM's version list at launch.

Pre-launch preparation you can do now

  1. Write your operations before you have data. Decide what you will buy, craft and sell per profession, and set price-threshold rules rather than fixed prices.
  2. Plan around First Aid potions. If you intend to sell healing, your leveling path should include First Aid and a spice supply chain.
  3. Decide your camp-object niche. Each profession has three objects at different skill levels; the first is learned at skill 20, the rest come from dungeon boss Blueprints.
  4. Budget for riding training across every character you intend to level, since it is now the main gold sink.
  5. Keep a manual price log from day one: item, date, min-buyout, quantity. Three weeks of your own data beats any borrowed spreadsheet.
  6. Re-verify tool support at launch. Addon compatibility is the single most likely thing to change between now and 4 November.

Before the first row is logged: the item column must hold an exact, verified in-game item name, and each new item gets its own row in the table above first — the market-log checker refuses a row whose item is not on this watchlist, and it refuses any row dated before 4 November 2026. Read a minimum buyout of one unit, in copper, record who observed it and when, and keep a screenshot or export permalink as the source. Nothing here is estimated, rounded or scraped.

Why we do not publish gold-per-hour numbers. Rates depend on the live economy, population and competition — none of which exist yet. Any figure published today would be invented, and invented numbers are how gold-making guides lose people money.
