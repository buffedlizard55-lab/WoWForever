How the guide sites are organised · snapshot 2026-09-20

Site patterns we verified, and the one that failed

The brief asks this project to reverse engineer portions of established WoW sites. The honest version of that is structure: which namespaces exist, how news is told apart from guides, how a Blizzard post is indexed, and where the sites that do cover Forever keep it. Every pattern below was confirmed by opening the pages — and the guess that did not survive verification is published too.

The answer to the question behind the request.

Wowhead and Icy Veins each run a dedicated Forever namespace — /forever/ and /wow-forever/ — and both keep news, guides and datamined material in separate sections a reader can tell apart. Skill Capped, the third site the brief names, has no Forever section at all: its live PvP tier list is retail Midnight Season 2 Solo Shuffle, because Forever launches without rated PvP to rank (C199).

What “reverse engineering” means here

A URL shape is public information. A guide's text is somebody's work. This project keeps the line between the two explicit, because that is the difference between learning how a site is built and taking it apart for parts.

The boundary this project holds to, recorded in data/patterns.js.

Read a page, note its URL shape, section namespaces, and how it separates news, guides and datamined material; credit it by name and link to it; use the structure to organise this site's own navigation. Scrape, mirror, rehost, translate or republish another site's content; paraphrase it at length; present its analysis as this project's finding; treat a guide as a primary source when a Blizzard page exists.

The result is a map, not a copy. Where this site has learned something about how a site organises Forever coverage, that is recorded with the date it was observed. Where it has learned something about the game, the claim is attributed to whoever published it and linked so a reader can check the original.

The worked example. This session guessed a plausible Icy Veins news address by shortening a headline and it returned a 404 page, while the real, registered URL for the same story exists and ends in a trailing slash. The lesson is not that Icy Veins broke a link — it is that a plausible slug is not a URL: news on that site has no numeric id, so the slug is the address, and inventing one produces a dead link that looks like a deleted article. Every URL on this site is copied from the site that serves it, and the failure is on the record rather than quietly retried until something returns 200.

What we take from them — and what we refuse to take

Taken: structure

  • The namespace split. A dedicated Forever section on both major sites is the clearest editorial statement available that a page is about this game and not the 2004 client — so this project's links are all drawn from those namespaces.
  • News versus guides. Dated posts and revised-in-place guides live in different sections, and this site's ledger keeps the same distinction by carrying a reading date on every source.
  • Visible caveats. Both sites label datamined material as changeable and mark unfinished sections as coming soon. This site's evidence classes and its “not announced” rows are the same discipline, written down.
  • Blizzard indexing. A tracker that mirrors Blizzard's news and forum posts is the fastest way to notice a statement, while the Blizzard page itself stays the citation.

Refused: conclusions

  • Their rankings. No tier list is copied, restated or used as a basis for one here; where a reputable site has declined to rank Forever classes, that refusal is itself the finding (C198).
  • Their data tables. Item, recipe, camp-object and set-bonus tables are summarised in this project's own words with the original linked, never lifted wholesale.
  • Their datamining. Client datamining is credited to the outlet that did it and tagged as changeable — this project does not strip a client and present the result as its own.
  • Anything unreadable. A page that blocks automated retrieval, or a URL that cannot be opened, produces a recorded gap rather than a summary of a summary.

Every source named on this page sits in the source registry with its publisher, the date it was read and the claims it supports, and it is fingerprinted weekly by the source-watch Action — so a site that reorganises, moves Forever to a new namespace or drops coverage entirely shows up as a dated issue rather than as a quietly stale link.
