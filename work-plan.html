Delivered so far

  • Sourced site covering roadmap, PvP, classes, gear/BiS status, gold and market mechanics, each on its own page.
  • Machine-readable source registry (data/sources.js) and claims ledger (data/claims.js) with evidence classes and snapshot dates.
  • Public irregularity log (I-1 to I-13) including fabricated tier-list detection, conflicting dungeon ranges, cross-version contamination on Forever item pages, the two-way mount-price reporting, interview “what ifs” circulating as roadmap items, and secondary reports that had been labelled too strongly as first-party.
  • Explicit gap documentation for every PvP, BiS and economy number that is still unpublished.
  • A no-hallucination contract: any statement without a source is either labelled our reasoning or moved to the “not announced” set.
  • Per-class PvP profiles — nine PvP-only profiles with an evidence chip and source link on every row, a one-line summary table, an explicit “no ranking” contract, and a verified per-class reading list (C111–C121, C138–C139).
  • Automated source watching — a weekly GitHub Action fingerprints every registered URL and opens an issue when a cited page changes or dies (tools/check-sources.mjs).
  • Four offline verification tools now gate the repository — tools/check-ledger.mjs (integrity, staleness), tools/check-citations.mjs (every external link must be a registered source, every internal anchor must resolve, every claim ID printed in prose must exist, tag balance), tools/check-market-log.mjs (market-log schema, with a hard refusal of any pre-launch price row) and tools/check-quotes.mjs (no quotation of three or more words may sit more than 1400 characters from a source link or a claim id). .github/workflows/verify.yml runs all four on every push and pull request, which is what makes the “fails the build” claim on this page true.
  • Market log scaffold — data/market-log.csv with a documented schema and deliberately zero data rows until an auction house exists, validated against the Gold page watchlist by the tool above.
  • Quotation audit and self-corrections — tools/check-quotes.mjs found 22 quotations with no source or claim id nearby; the four real ones were anchored and the rest allow-listed as interface labels. The human half of the same check (re-reading borrowed wording against the live page) found and fixed a quotation that did not exist in its source, a coverage claim that understated another site's output, and provenance labels that overstated secondary evidence — I-11, I-12 and I-13, all logged rather than quietly rewritten.
  • Machine-checked citation rule — the registry now supports multi-page sources (also: []), so a page cannot link to a URL that no one registered and read. The first run of the checker found 18 unregistered outbound links on five pages; all 18 were resolved by registering the sources rather than by deleting the links.
  • Primary-source gate — official is now reserved for a Blizzard-hosted URL with firstParty: true; secondary reports of Blizzard statements are labelled Press. The ledger fails an official claim that has no first-party source, and the direct Krol’dok and beta known-issues forum posts are registered and read, with future manual review still required when they change.
  • All five open project decisions taken and implemented; see Decisions.

Phase 2 — launch window, 4 November onward

Data pipeline

  • Stand up the level-60 item verification pipeline described on the Gear page: item ID, stat block, drop source, phase, permalink, verification date.
  • Publish pre-raid gear tables per class, split by armour type and role, with acquisition links only from the Forever namespace.
  • Begin the market baseline log: daily minimum buyouts for the watchlist items on the Gold page, with the 100 g / 1,000 g training tiers as the calendar anchors.
  • Record which price addons actually function on Forever clients, then document a verified workflow.

PvP work

  • Record battleground availability the moment the live client confirms it, replacing the current “implied only” status — the beta article already confirms a new battleground enters testing during the beta, so the first observation may arrive before launch.
  • Collect Honor gain observations from real battlegrounds to replace the unpublished numbers with measured ones — clearly labelled as observed, not official.
  • Stand up battleground-role notes per class as play begins, using the published methodology on the Classes page.

Phase 3 — raid window, 9 December onward

  • First defensible BiS lists — pre-raid and phase-1 raid sets, each item source-verified, with the previous version retained.
  • Crafted tier-set economy — track Malleable Essence of Nature supply, Guardians of Hyjal reputation gating, and the BoE crafted-piece market as it forms.
  • Chase-item tracking — every boss keeps low-drop-rate items that Blizzard intends to stay useful in later tiers, so the tracker keeps a per-boss “still worth farming” column rather than closing a tier when the set is complete.
  • First PvP value assessment — battleground-role based, minimum-sample enforced, version-stamped, with the evidence for every placement.
  • Legendary and chase-item documentation — Blizzard has teased a legendary reward and every raid boss having chase items; document them when they are live.

The five decisions, and their follow-through Project policy

The five open decisions were taken on 2026-09-18 and implemented in the same session; the second column below records what the following session actually did with each one. These are editorial policies, not game facts, and they are in the ledger as claim C122 so the site's own rules are auditable alongside its claims.

Decisions taken, the artefact that implements each one, and what the third pass added.
DecisionChosenWhat shippedFollow-through this session
Launch-coverage aggressiveness
Verified sources only, or also community-reported drop rates?		 (a) Verification first, with community-reported data admitted later only behind a visible reported tag and never mixed into a verified table. The rule is enforced by the ledger: a claim needs a registered source and an evidence class, and tools/check-ledger.mjs fails the build if either is missing. Strengthened from a rule into a machine check: tools/check-citations.mjs now fails the build if any page links to a URL that is not in the registry, and it found 18 such links on five pages in its first run. All 18 were registered with publisher, date and tier instead of being deleted. The community class is still unused for game facts, which is the honest state of play before launch.
Scheduled URL-watch Action
Manual re-checking, or automated polling?		 (b) Add the Action. It checks whether cited pages moved; it never edits site content. .github/workflows/source-watch.yml runs every Monday at 07:00 UTC (and on demand), fingerprints every registered URL with volatile chrome stripped, and opens an issue listing changed and unreachable sources. The watcher stayed as designed, and a second workflow (verify.yml) was added so the ledger, citation and market-log checks run on every push and pull request as well. The watcher still cannot publish or re-date a claim — that boundary is unchanged.
PvP tier-list timing
Wait for a season, or publish provisionally after launch week?		 (a) Wait for a full season. No tier, grade or score is published before then, under any label. The Class PvP profiles page ships the useful half of a tier list — published, sourced, battleground-relevant mechanics per class — with an explicit “no ranking” contract. Held. The new material strengthened the profiles instead of tempting a ranking: nine per-class rows now carry a per-spec, per-class reading list, and C138 records that the only public build view is structural. A separate ledger row (C140) exists purely so a “Caverns of Time battleground” wish cannot leak into the PvP page as a plan.
Market-logging effort
Manual daily log, volunteers, or wait for tooling?		 (a) Manual, watchlist only. Small, bounded, and verifiable by one person. data/market-log.csv exists with a documented schema and zero data rows, because no auction house exists before 4 November 2026. Made auditable: tools/check-market-log.mjs validates the schema, refuses any observation dated before launch or in the future, requires an observer on every row, and cross-checks item names against the Gold page watchlist. The largest gold finding of the project landed here too — 100 g / 1,000 g riding training (C127) — and the catalyst table now dates both gold-sink waves.
Scope of class profiles
PvE and PvP, or PvP-only?		 (b) PvP-only, with PvE notes only where they affect gearing or are the source of the ability described. Nine profiles, one per class, each row carrying an evidence chip and a source link, plus a one-line summary table and a per-class “still unknown” column. Held, and extended: the profile page gained a verified reading list per class, and the Classes page corrected its description of Wowhead's talent calculator after the re-check showed the underlying data is BlizzCon demo material, not beta-client extraction.

Why the defaults were adopted rather than overridden. Four of the five defaults were the conservative option and nothing found in either verification pass argued against them. The one that required approval — the scheduled Action — was adopted because it only detects change and files an issue for a human; it cannot publish, edit or re-date a claim. That boundary is what makes automation compatible with a no-hallucination rule.
